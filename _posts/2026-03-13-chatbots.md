Recently I have been plagued by the question: “Why do LLM-based chatbots sometimes sound ‘schizophrenic?’”

In exploring a “Choose-Your-Own-Adventure“ style prompt in a fantasy setting inspired heavily by the works of Jack Vance, just as a way to kill some time, I began to notice patterns in the chatbot’s manner of speaking:

> The cylinder goes somewhere safe — a pocket, a fold, wherever such things are carried by people who have accepted them. It settles there as though it has always been there.

> It regards you with the specific quality of attention of something that is not performing interest because it does not need to.

> There is a quality of stillness to it that is not the stillness of stone or sleep but of something that has simply stopped requiring movement.

> …seated, still, present in the way of someone who has been waiting with complete patience for a specific and expected arrival.

> He says nothing further. He doesn't need to.

These snippets are taken from two chats and are not presented in chronological order. What common threads do you notice? What I began to pick up on was the idea of objects or things or people "not needing to" do things as an indication of intentionality. Or, in the first—in my view most glaring—example, an object nestling into a pocket "as though it had always been there." These turns of phrase rubbed me the wrong way, so I decided to sit down and think about why.
